Open any kitchen cabinet in a typical apartment and you'll probably see the same thing: a short stack of plates or a row of mugs, with a big empty gap above them. That empty air is storage you're paying rent for but not using.

Shelf risers fix it. They're small freestanding shelves that sit inside a cabinet and create a second level. They're cheap, require no tools, and they're completely renter-friendly.

## Why shelf risers work

Fixed cabinet shelves are often spaced 10–14 inches apart, while a stack of dinner plates might be only 4–5 inches tall. A riser lets you split that height in two. In practice, it can turn one crowded shelf into two organized ones — with nothing stacked so high that it's hard to reach.

## How to measure for shelf risers

Before buying, measure:

1. **Interior width** of the cabinet (wall to wall, inside the frame)
2. **Depth** from the back wall to the inside of the door — subtract room for hinges
3. **Height** between the shelves
4. **The height of what will go under the riser** — like a stack of bowls — plus about half an inch of clearance

Doors and face frames can make the opening narrower than the inside of the cabinet, so check that the riser will actually fit through the door.

## Types of shelf risers

### Wire shelf risers

The most common type. Coated wire is lightweight, inexpensive, and lets you see what's underneath. Good for plates, bowls and mugs.

## Where to use shelf risers

### Dish cabinets

Store dinner plates on top of the riser and bowls or side plates underneath. Or put salad plates on top and dinner plates below if you use them more.

### Mug cabinets

Mugs are short, so a cabinet often has half its height empty. One riser doubles the number of mugs you can fit in a single row. You can even hang mugs under some risers with hooks.

### Pantry shelves

Use tiered risers for cans and jars so you can see what's in the back row. Use a flat riser to store snack boxes on top and canned goods below.

### Under the sink and bathroom vanities

A riser can create a second level for cleaning supplies or toiletries, as long as it's rust-resistant and you work around the pipes.

### Countertop corners

A riser in a countertop corner can hold a fruit bowl on top and spices or oils underneath — making use of awkward corner space.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Not leaving clearance.** If you have to tilt a bowl to get it out, the riser is too low.
- **Overloading the top.** Heavy stoneware plates can bow cheap risers. Check the weight capacity.
- **Using too many risers.** Risers work best when each holds one clear category. Stacking random items on multiple levels just creates layered clutter.
- **Forgetting the door.** Make sure the riser isn't so deep that it blocks the door.

## Pair risers with other cabinet organizers

- **Lazy Susans** for deep corners
- **Door racks** for lids and cutting boards
- **Clear bins** to pull out groups of items like baking supplies
- **Pot lid organizers** in lower cabinets

## The bottom line

Shelf risers are one of the simplest, cheapest small-space upgrades you can make. Measure carefully, choose the style that fits each cabinet, and give each level one job. In under an hour, you can effectively double the usable space in your kitchen cabinets.
