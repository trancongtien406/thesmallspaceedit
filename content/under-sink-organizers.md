The cabinet under the sink is usually the most chaotic spot in any home. It's dark, deep, awkwardly shaped around plumbing, and it collects every cleaning product you've ever bought. In a small apartment, though, it's also valuable storage you can't afford to waste.

Here's how to turn it into an organized, easy-to-use space — without any permanent changes to the cabinet.

## Step 1: Empty it completely and check for leaks

Before you organize, take everything out. Use this moment to:

- **Look for signs of leaks** — water stains, warped cabinet floors, or a musty smell. If you find any, report them to your landlord before you load the space up again.
- **Wipe the cabinet floor** and let it dry completely.
- **Toss duplicates and dried-out products.** Most people find two or three half-used bottles of the same cleaner.

## Step 2: Measure around the plumbing

Under-sink cabinets are tricky because the drain pipe and garbage disposal (if you have one) take up the middle. Measure:

1. The total interior width, depth and height
2. The width of the space on each side of the pipes
3. The height from the cabinet floor to the bottom of the drain trap

These numbers tell you whether a single wide organizer will fit or whether you need two narrow ones on either side.

## Step 3: Choose the right organizer type

### Two-tier pull-out (sliding) organizers

These are the best upgrade for most under-sink cabinets. Each tier slides out like a drawer, so you can reach things at the back without kneeling and digging. Many have narrow footprints designed to sit on either side of the pipes, and some have adjustable heights.

::: product Two-tier sliding under-sink organizer | Pull-out drawers that work around plumbing.

### A tension rod for spray bottles

A simple tension rod placed across the top of the cabinet lets you hang spray bottles by their triggers. That frees up a surprising amount of floor space and keeps your most-used sprays at eye level when you open the door.

::: product Adjustable tension rod | Hang spray bottles by their triggers to free up floor space.

### Clear bins with handles

Group similar items — dish soap refills, sponges, dishwasher pods, trash bags — in clear bins. Handles make it easy to pull the whole bin out at once. Measure the depth so bins don't block the cabinet door from closing.

::: product Clear storage bins with handles | Group like items and pull them out in one go.

### Door-mounted caddies

The inside of the cabinet door can hold sponges, brushes, rubber gloves or a roll of trash bags. Adhesive or over-the-door caddies work well here — just check that the caddy won't hit the pipes when the door closes.

::: product Over-cabinet-door caddy | Uses the inside of the door for sponges and gloves.

### A drip tray or waterproof liner

A waterproof cabinet mat or drip tray protects the cabinet floor from small spills and gives you early warning of leaks. It's one of the cheapest ways to protect your security deposit.

::: product Waterproof under-sink drip tray | Protects the cabinet floor and catches small leaks early.

## Step 4: Arrange by frequency of use

- **Front and center (or on the top tier):** dish soap refills, sponges, everyday spray cleaner
- **Hanging on the tension rod:** spray bottles
- **Back or bottom tier:** backup supplies, specialty cleaners
- **On the door:** gloves, brushes, small items

## Safety tips

- **Keep chemicals away from children and pets.** If you have little ones, add an adhesive childproof cabinet latch — many are removable when you move out.
- **Never store bleach and ammonia products next to each other** in open containers, and keep lids tightly closed.
- **Don't block the shut-off valves.** You (or a plumber) need quick access to them in an emergency.

## Bathroom under-sink storage

The same principles apply to bathroom vanities. A two-tier sliding organizer can hold hair tools and backup toiletries, clear bins can corral first-aid supplies, and a door caddy can hold a hair dryer or brushes. Bathroom vanities are often shallower, so double-check depth.

## Keep it organized long-term

- **Do a 5-minute reset once a month.** Pull everything out of one tier, wipe it, and put back only what belongs.
- **Buy refills only when the bin is low.** Clear containers make this easy.
- **Label the bins** if several people share the space.

## The bottom line

Your under-sink cabinet doesn't have to be a black hole. Measure around the pipes, add a pull-out organizer and a tension rod, and group the rest in clear bins. It's an hour of work that makes every cleanup faster for months.
